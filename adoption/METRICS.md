# Adoption Metrics

Adoption Metrics measure whether a standard has successfully transitioned from
approval to lived practice. They provide objective evidence for the Review stage
and help identify where support or adjustments are needed.

Metrics are not optional — they are the backbone of accountable governance.

---

## Purpose

- Track adoption progress across teams and systems  
- Identify blockers and lagging areas  
- Provide evidence for scheduled reviews  
- Ensure standards become lived practice  

---

## Core Metrics

### 1. Adoption Coverage
Percentage of teams or systems that have implemented the standard.

### 2. Migration Progress
Percentage of required migration steps completed.

### 3. Enforcement Compliance
Rate of CI/linter violations over time.

### 4. Exception Requests
Number and type of exceptions requested by teams.

### 5. Time to Adoption
Elapsed time from enforcement readiness to full adoption.

### 6. Drift Indicators
Instances where teams deviate from the standard after adoption.

---

## Reporting Cadence

- Monthly during active rollout  
- Quarterly after adoption stabilizes  
- Prior to every scheduled Review cycle  

---

## Data Sources

- CI/linter logs  
- Team reports  
- Migration guides  
- Rollout plans  
- Exception logs  

---

## Steward Responsibilities

- Aggregate and publish adoption metrics  
- Identify systemic blockers  
- Escalate risks to leadership or working groups  
- Use metrics to inform Review decisions  

---

## Anti‑Patterns

- “Adoption by assumption” (no metrics)  
- Reporting only successes, not blockers  
- Treating adoption as a one‑time event  
- Ignoring drift after initial rollout  

---

## Success Criteria

A standard is considered **adopted** when:

- Adoption coverage exceeds the defined threshold  
- Migration is complete or on track  
- Enforcement violations trend downward  
- No unresolved systemic blockers remain
