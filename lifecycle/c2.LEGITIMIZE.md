# **Legitimize Stage**

The Legitimize stage is where a proposed standard is subjected to **structured, transparent, evidence‑based review**.  
It is the constitutional safeguard that prevents unclear, unenforceable, or misaligned standards from entering the system.

Legitimize is not a formality.  
It is the **gatekeeper of quality, coherence, and long‑term maintainability**.

---

## **Purpose**

- Ensure the proposed standard is **correct, complete, and coherent**  
- Validate alignment with existing standards, principles, and architectural boundaries  
- Expose risks, conflicts, and unintended consequences  
- Confirm that the standard is **enforceable** and **adoptable**  
- Produce a **recorded decision** with rationale  

Legitimize transforms a draft into a **governance‑approved specification**.

---

## **Scope**

Legitimize applies to:

- All new standards  
- All revisions to existing standards  
- All deprecations  
- Any rule that will be enforced mechanically or expected across teams  

If it affects more than one team, it must be legitimized.

---

# **Sub‑Stages**

## **L1. Reviewer Assignment**

The Steward assigns Reviewers who collectively represent:

- Domain expertise  
- Cross‑team perspective  
- Architectural awareness  
- UX, security, or compliance considerations (as needed)  

**Reviewers must be independent of the Author’s team** to avoid bias.

---

## **L2. Structured Review**

Reviewers evaluate the proposal using:

- The Reviewer Checklist  
- The lifecycle principles  
- Existing standards and architectural constraints  
- Evidence from Codify (impact analysis, rationale, alternatives)  

Review must be:

- **Evidence‑based**  
- **Traceable**  
- **Bounded in scope**  
- **Focused on correctness, clarity, and enforceability**  

Reviewers do not rewrite the proposal — they evaluate it.

---

## **L3. Decision**

A decision must be one of:

- **Approve**  
- **Approve with Changes**  
- **Reject**  
- **Table** (insufficient information; return to Codify)  

Every decision must include:

- Rationale  
- Required changes (if any)  
- Concerns raised  
- Evidence considered  

The decision becomes part of the permanent governance record.

---

# **Required Artifacts**

Every Legitimize stage must produce:

- **Reviewer Checklist** (completed)  
- **Review Notes** (per reviewer)  
- **Decision Record** (ADR‑style)  
- **Finalized Specification** (post‑revision)  

These artifacts ensure transparency and traceability.

---

# **Roles and Responsibilities**

## **Reviewers (Lead)**

- Evaluate the proposal against principles, standards, and constraints  
- Provide clear, actionable, evidence‑based feedback  
- Identify risks, conflicts, and missing rationale  
- Ensure the proposal is enforceable and adoptable  
- Record their reasoning transparently  

Reviewers are the **guardians of quality**.

---

## **Governance Steward (Facilitator)**

- Ensures the review follows the lifecycle  
- Prevents scope creep and bikeshedding  
- Confirms that all required artifacts are produced  
- Ensures the decision is recorded and justified  
- Protects the integrity of the process  

The Steward is the **protector of governance discipline**.

---

## **Author (Responder)**

- Responds to reviewer feedback  
- Clarifies intent and rationale  
- Revises the proposal as needed  
- Ensures the final specification reflects agreed changes  

The Author is responsible for **closing the loop**.

---

# **Entry and Exit Criteria**

## **Entry Criteria**

- Complete Codify artifacts  
- Steward confirms review‑readiness  
- Reviewers assigned  

## **Exit Criteria**

- Decision recorded with rationale  
- All required artifacts completed  
- Finalized specification approved  
- Proposal ready for **Enforce**  

If any criteria are unmet, the proposal remains in Legitimize.

---

# **Quality Bar**

A proposal is **legitimized** when:

- Reviewers unanimously agree it is clear, correct, and enforceable  
- All concerns have been addressed  
- No contradictions with existing standards remain  
- Enforcement is technically feasible  
- Migration is plausible  
- The specification is stable and unambiguous  

If Reviewers cannot agree, the proposal returns to Codify.

---

# **Anti‑Patterns**

- **Rubber‑stamping:** Approving without deep evaluation  
- **Bikeshedding:** Debating trivial details instead of core issues  
- **Silent approval:** No explicit decision or rationale  
- **Reviewer overreach:** Reviewers rewriting the proposal  
- **Unbounded review:** Endless feedback loops without closure  
- **Ignoring enforceability:** Approving rules that cannot be automated  
- **Ignoring adoption:** Approving rules that teams cannot realistically implement  

These patterns undermine governance integrity.

Only when all boxes are checked may the proposal advance.
