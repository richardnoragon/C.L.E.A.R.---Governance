# Codify stage

The Codify stage transforms intent into a clear, rationale‑driven, reviewable artifact.  
Nothing moves into governance until it has been **written down, justified, and scoped**.

---

## Purpose

- **Make intent explicit:** Turn vague needs, frustrations, or ideas into a concrete problem statement.  
- **Anchor decisions in rationale:** Capture *why* a standard is needed and *why this approach*.  
- **Prepare for review:** Produce artifacts that allow Reviewers to evaluate, not reconstruct, the proposal.

Codify is where governance debt is either prevented—or created.

---

## Scope

Codify applies to:

- New standards (technical, UX, process, architectural)  
- Revisions to existing standards  
- Deprecation proposals for existing standards  
- Cross‑team rules that affect more than one application or domain  

If it will be enforced or expected of others, it must pass through Codify.

---

## Sub‑stages

### C1. Intent capture

- **Goal:** Capture the problem, not the solution.  
- **Outputs:**
  - Problem statement  
  - Context and constraints  
  - Stakeholders affected  

**Questions to answer:**

- What is broken, unclear, inconsistent, or risky today?  
- Who is affected, and how?  
- What constraints are non‑negotiable (security, compliance, performance, UX, etc.)?

---

### C2. Draft specification

- **Goal:** Propose a concrete, testable standard.  
- **Outputs:**
  - Draft specification (the “rule”)  
  - Goals and non‑goals  
  - Rationale  
  - Alternatives considered  

**Characteristics of a good draft:**

- Uses **normative language**: *MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, MAY* where appropriate.  
- Is **implementation‑agnostic** where possible, but **enforceable** in practice.  
- Is **scoped**: clearly states what is in and out of scope.

---

### C3. Impact and migration analysis

- **Goal:** Understand the blast radius and feasibility.  
- **Outputs:**
  - Impact analysis (teams, systems, workflows)  
  - Initial migration considerations  
  - Risks and trade‑offs  

**Questions to answer:**

- Which systems or teams will be affected?  
- What existing patterns will this standard replace or constrain?  
- What are the likely objections or risks?

---

## Required artifacts

Every Codify output **must** include at least:

- **Problem statement**  
- **Goals and non‑goals**  
- **Rationale**  
- **Proposed specification**  
- **Alternatives considered**  
- **Impact analysis**  
- **Initial migration considerations**  

These map directly onto your `STANDARD_TEMPLATE.md`.

---

## Roles and responsibilities

### Author (Lead)

- Owns the draft and its evolution during Codify.  
- Ensures all required artifacts are present and coherent.  
- Engages Domain SMEs for correctness and feasibility.

### Governance Steward (Facilitator)

- Ensures the draft **meets lifecycle expectations** before entering Legitimize.  
- Challenges ambiguity, missing rationale, and unclear scope.  
- Confirms that the proposal belongs at the governance level (not just team‑local).

### Domain SMEs (Support)

- Validate technical, UX, or process correctness.  
- Highlight hidden constraints and edge cases.  
- Identify potential conflicts with existing standards.

---

## Entry and exit criteria

### Entry criteria

- A recognized need for a standard, revision, or deprecation.  
- An **assigned Author** and **identified Steward**.

### Exit criteria

- All required artifacts are present and internally consistent.  
- The specification is **review‑ready**: clear, scoped, and enforceable in principle.  
- The Steward agrees that the proposal can move to **Legitimize**.

If any of these are missing, the proposal remains in Codify.

---

## Quality bar

A Codify output is **ready** when:

- Someone unfamiliar with the context can understand:
  - What is being proposed  
  - Why it exists  
  - What it affects  
- The standard can plausibly be:
  - Enforced mechanically  
  - Adopted with a migration plan  
  - Reviewed against clear goals  

If Reviewers must reconstruct intent or rationale, Codify has not done its job.

---

## Anti‑patterns

- **Solution in search of a problem:** No clear problem statement.  
- **Hand‑wavy language:** “Try to”, “prefer to”, “it would be nice if…”.  
- **Hidden constraints:** Important context lives in chat, not in the spec.  
- **Over‑scoping:** One standard tries to solve five unrelated problems.  
- **Under‑scoping:** A “standard” that only applies to one team’s edge case.  
- **No alternatives:** Pretending there was only one possible approach.  
- **Unenforceable rules:** Standards that cannot be checked mechanically.

---

## Checklist for “Codify complete”

- [ ] Problem statement is explicit and concrete  
- [ ] Goals and non‑goals are listed  
- [ ] Rationale explains *why this* and *why now*  
- [ ] Specification uses clear, normative language  
- [ ] Alternatives considered are documented  
- [ ] Impact analysis covers teams, systems, and risks  
- [ ] Initial migration considerations are present  
- [ ] Steward has reviewed and agrees it is ready for Legitimize  

Only when all boxes are checked should the proposal move to the **Legitimize** stage.
