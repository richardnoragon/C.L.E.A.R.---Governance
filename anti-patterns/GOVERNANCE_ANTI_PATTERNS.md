# Governance Anti‑Patterns

Governance anti‑patterns are systemic behaviors that weaken clarity, undermine accountability, and erode the integrity of the C.L.E.A.R. lifecycle.  
This document identifies the most common governance failures and how to prevent them.

---

## 1. Skipping Lifecycle Stages
Moving a proposal directly from idea → approval without Codify or Legitimize.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Creates unclear, unenforceable, or contradictory standards.

**Prevention:**  
Steward enforces lifecycle discipline.

---

## 2. Silent Approval
Approving a proposal without documented rationale or reviewer notes.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Destroys traceability and weakens institutional memory.

**Prevention:**  
Require explicit decisions and rationale for every review.

---

## 3. Governance by Chat
Decisions made informally in Slack, Teams, or hallway conversations.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Creates invisible rules and inconsistent expectations.

**Prevention:**  
All decisions must be recorded in the repository.

---

## 4. Over‑Centralization
Steward or reviewers acting as gatekeepers for personal preference.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Stifles collaboration and introduces bias.

**Prevention:**  
Roles must follow the charter, not personal taste.

---

## 5. Under‑Governance
Allowing teams to create their own local rules without alignment.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Leads to fragmentation, duplication, and drift.

**Prevention:**  
Standards must be cross‑team unless explicitly scoped otherwise.

---

## 6. Governance Drift
Standards that evolve informally without review or documentation.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Breaks consistency and invalidates enforcement.

**Prevention:**  
Scheduled Review is mandatory.

---

## 7. Enforcement Without Legitimacy
Implementing CI rules or linters before a standard is approved.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Creates shadow governance.

**Prevention:**  
Enforcement only begins after Legitimize.

---

## 8. Decision Hoarding
Delaying decisions indefinitely due to fear of being wrong.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Blocks progress and increases governance debt.

**Prevention:**  
Use the Review cycle to correct mistakes later.

---

## 9. Ambiguous Ownership
No clear assignment of Author, Steward, or Reviewers.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Creates confusion and stalls progress.

**Prevention:**  
Roles must be explicitly assigned at each stage.

---

## 10. Standards Without Adoption
Approving standards that never become lived practice.

**Why it’s harmful:**  
Creates a false sense of governance maturity.

**Prevention:**  
Adopt stage is mandatory and measurable.
