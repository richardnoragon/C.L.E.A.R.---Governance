# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

This FAQ addresses common questions about the C.L.E.A.R. governance system, its
lifecycle, and its roles.

---

## What is the purpose of the C.L.E.A.R. governance system?
To ensure that standards are created, validated, enforced, adopted, and reviewed
in a deterministic, transparent, and role‑anchored manner.

---

## Why does every standard need to go through all five lifecycle stages?
Skipping stages leads to unclear, unenforceable, or unadoptable standards.
The lifecycle ensures quality, consistency, and accountability.

---

## Who can propose a new standard?
Anyone may propose a standard, but an **Author** must be assigned to draft it and
a **Steward** must oversee its progression.

---

## What makes a standard “enforceable”?
A standard is enforceable when it can be validated mechanically through CI,
linters, scaffolds, or reference implementations.

---

## What happens if a standard becomes outdated?
It enters the **Review** stage, where it may be reaffirmed, revised, replaced, or
retired based on evidence and feedback.

---

## How often are standards reviewed?
Each standard defines its own review cadence.  
If unspecified, the default is **every 12 months**.

---

## What if a team cannot adopt a standard?
Teams must document blockers and escalate them through the Steward.  
Exceptions may be granted, but they must be justified and traceable.

---

## What is the difference between a Rollout Plan and a Migration Guide?
- **Rollout Plan:** How the standard is introduced across teams.  
- **Migration Guide:** How each team implements the standard.

---

## Who ensures that governance is followed correctly?
The **Governance Steward** ensures lifecycle integrity, traceability, and
compliance.

---

## Can standards be experimental?
Yes. Experimental standards follow the same lifecycle but may have shorter review
cycles and more flexible enforcement.
